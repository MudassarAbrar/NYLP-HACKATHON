PART 1 - CORE PUNJAB WOMEN-PROTECTION LAWS
----------------------------------------------------------------

1. PUNJAB PROTECTION OF WOMEN AGAINST VIOLENCE ACT, 2016
   Act XVI of 2016
   Official full text source:
   https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/natlex2/files/download/102088/PAKD102088.pdf

   Scope:
   Protection, relief and rehabilitation of women against violence,
   including domestic violence, sexual violence, psychological abuse,
   economic abuse, stalking and cybercrime.

   Key sections:
   - Section 1: Short title, extent and commencement.
   - Section 2: Definitions - aggrieved person, defendant, domestic
     violence, economic abuse, psychological violence, stalking,
     cybercrime, shelter home, Protection Centre.
   - Section 3: Measures for implementation - helpline, Protection
     Centres, shelter homes, staff, awareness and database.
   - Section 4: Complaint to Court - complaint by aggrieved woman,
     authorized person, or Women Protection Officer; first hearing
     within seven days; final decision within ninety days.
   - Section 5: Right to reside in house - protection from unlawful
     eviction; alternative accommodation or shelter home.
   - Section 6: Interim order - urgent protection during proceedings.
   - Section 7: Protection order - no contact, stay-away order,
     GPS tracker in grave cases, surrender of weapons, restrictions
     on entering workplace or other places.
   - Section 8: Residence order - right to remain in shared house,
     protection from eviction, alternative accommodation, return of
     property/documents.
   - Section 9: Monetary order - compensation, medical expenses,
     loss of earnings, shelter costs, maintenance-related relief.
   - Section 10: Duration and alteration of orders.
   - Section 11: District Women Protection Committee.
   - Section 12: Functions of the Committee.
   - Section 13: Protection Centres and shelter homes - FIR, medical
     examination, forensic evidence, counselling, legal aid, shelter.
   - Section 14: Women Protection Officer - rescue, counselling,
     coordination, support and protection duties.
   - Section 15: Power to enter a house/place to rescue an aggrieved
     woman, with safeguards and consent requirements.
   - Section 16: Assistance on request.
   - Section 17: Assistance to officers by police and other agencies.
   - Section 18: Penalty for obstructing a Protection Officer.
   - Section 19: Penalty for filing false complaint.
   - Section 20: Penalty for breach of orders - up to one year
     imprisonment and/or Rs 50,000-200,000 fine; repeated breach may
     bring one to two years and/or Rs 100,000-500,000 fine.
   - Section 21: Cognizance and summary trial.
   - Section 22: Appeal to Court of Session within thirty days.
   - Section 23: Right to information, subject to privacy safeguards.
   - Section 24: Certain persons to be public servants.
   - Section 25: Performance audit.
   - Section 26: Act not in derogation of other laws.
   - Section 27: Training.
   - Section 28: Annual report.
   - Section 29: Rules.
   - Section 30: Immunity for good-faith actions.
   - Section 31: Power to remove difficulties.

2. PUNJAB WOMEN PROTECTION AUTHORITY ACT, 2017
   Source:
   https://jppbenchbook.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/The-Punjab-Women-Protection-Authority-Act-2017.pdf

   Scope:
   Establishes the Punjab Women Protection Authority to implement,
   coordinate, monitor and improve women-protection mechanisms.

   Key areas:
   - Establishment and composition of the Authority.
   - Policy-making, monitoring and coordination functions.
   - District Women Protection Committees.
   - Coordination with police, health, social welfare, judiciary
     and local government.
   - Rule-making and implementation powers.

3. PUNJAB COMMISSION ON THE STATUS OF WOMEN ACT, 2014
   Source:
   https://pcsw.punjab.gov.pk/womens_rights

   Scope:
   Institutional body for policy advice, monitoring, research and
   protection of women's rights in Punjab.

   Key areas:
   - Review of laws and policies affecting women.
   - Monitoring implementation of women-protection laws.
   - Research, recommendations and awareness.
   - Coordination with government departments.

4. PUNJAB FAIR REPRESENTATION OF WOMEN ACT, 2014
   Source:
   https://pcsw.punjab.gov.pk/womens_rights

   Scope:
   Promotes women's representation in public institutions and
   decision-making bodies.

   Key areas:
   - Minimum representation requirements for women.
   - Appointment and nomination safeguards.
   - Monitoring women's participation in public institutions.

PART 2 - HARASSMENT AND WORKPLACE PROTECTION

5. PROTECTION AGAINST HARASSMENT OF WOMEN AT THE WORKPLACE ACT, 2010
   As amended in 2022
   Official source:
   https://fospah.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/PAHAW_amend_2022.pdf

   Scope:
   Applies to public and private workplaces, educational institutions
   and other organizations throughout Pakistan, including Punjab.

   Key sections:
   - Section 2: Definitions - harassment, workplace, employee,
     employer, complainant, respondent.
   - Section 2A / 3: Prohibited conduct and scope - unwelcome sexual
     advances, requests for sexual favours, verbal, non-verbal,
     written or physical conduct creating a hostile environment.
   - Section 3: Mandatory Inquiry Committee in every organization.
   - Section 4: Competent authority and employer responsibilities.
   - Section 5: Inquiry procedure - complaint, inquiry, confidentiality,
     opportunity to respond.
   - Section 6: Powers of Inquiry Committee.
   - Section 7: Penalties and disciplinary action.
   - Section 8: Complaint to Ombudsperson where the complainant is
     dissatisfied or no committee exists.
   - Sections 9-12: Ombudsperson procedure, appeals, confidentiality
     and implementation.

   Forum:
   - Workplace Inquiry Committee first.
   - FOSPAH directly if no committee exists, there is delay, bias,
     or dissatisfaction.
   - FOSPAH helpline: 051-9218845
   - Email: complaints@fospah.gov.pk

7. PAKISTAN PENAL CODE, 1860 (PPC)
   Official source:
   https://pakistancode.gov.pk/pdffiles/administratord5622ea3f15bfa00b17d2cf7770a8434.pdf

   Women-relevant sections:

   - Section 294: Obscene acts and songs in or near a public place.
   - Section 324: Attempted murder; relevant to serious acid attacks
     and violent assaults.
   - Section 326 / 326A-326B: Causing hurt by dangerous weapons or
     means; acid-related provisions.
   - Section 337: Hurt; relevant to physical assault and injury.
   - Section 337E: Acid crime provisions.
   - Section 354: Assault or criminal force to woman with intent to
     outrage her modesty.
   - Section 354A: Assault or use of criminal force to woman and
     stripping her of clothes.
   - Section 354B: Vitiating atmosphere for woman.
   - Section 354C: Voyeurism - watching or capturing private acts
     without consent.
   - Section 354D: Stalking, including electronic stalking.
   - Section 365-365B: Kidnapping and abduction, including abduction
     for marriage or illicit purposes.
   - Section 366A: Procuration of minor girl.
   - Section 366B: Importation of girl from foreign country.
   - Section 375-376: Rape and punishment for rape.
   - Section 376A-376E: Aggravated rape-related offences, including
     gang rape and custodial rape.
   - Section 493: Cohabitation caused by deceitful means.
   - Section 494: Bigamy.
   - Section 495: Concealment of former marriage.
   - Section 496: Marriage ceremony fraudulently gone through without
     lawful marriage.
   - Section 496A: Fictitious marriage ceremony.
   - Section 497: Adultery-related provision; legal treatment has
     changed through judicial interpretation and later law.
   - Section 498: Enticing, taking away or detaining a married woman
     with criminal intent.
   - Section 498A: Kidnapping or abducting woman to compel marriage.
   - Section 498B: Prohibition of forced marriage.
       Punishment: 3-7 years imprisonment and fine up to Rs 500,000.
       For a girl child or non-Muslim woman: 5-10 years and fine up
       to Rs 1,000,000.
   - Section 506: Criminal intimidation; relevant to threats and
     blackmail.
   - Section 509: Insulting modesty, sexual harassment and related
     conduct.
   - Section 499-500: Defamation; relevant to false accusations and
     reputation attacks.
   - Section 463-477A: Forgery and forged documents; relevant to fake
     nikahnama, fake divorce papers, fake property documents and
     fraudulent transfers.

PART 4 - ONLINE CRIME, FAKE PICTURES, DEEPFAKES AND BLACKMAIL
----------------------------------------------------------------

12. PREVENTION OF ELECTRONIC CRIMES ACT, 2016 (PECA)
    Official source:
    https://www.na.gov.pk/uploads/documents/1470910659_707.pdf

    Scope:
    Federal cybercrime law applying in Punjab; main law for online
    harassment, image-based abuse, deepfakes and cyberstalking.

    Key sections:

    - Section 16: Unauthorized access to information systems.
    - Section 17: Unauthorized copying or transmission of data.
    - Section 18: Interference with information systems.
    - Section 19: Unauthorized access to critical infrastructure.
    - Section 20: Cyberstalking / harassment through information
      systems.
    - Section 21: Offences against modesty of a natural person and
      minor.
        Covers non-consensual intimate images/videos, sexually
        explicit content, superimposing a face onto explicit material
        (deepfake), and blackmail using intimate images.
    - Section 22: Hate speech.
    - Section 24: Cyberstalking - repeated electronic contact,
      monitoring, tracking, following and intimidation.
    - Section 25: Spamming.
    - Section 26: Spoofing - fake caller IDs, fake profiles and
      impersonation.
    - Section 27: Unauthorized interception of transmission.
    - Section 28: Cyber terrorism.
    - Section 29: Electronic forgery - fake electronic documents,
      fake IDs, deceptive screenshots.
    - Section 30: Electronic fraud - financial scams and fraudulent
      accounts.
    - Section 37: Power to remove or block unlawful content.
    - Section 38: Preservation and collection of digital evidence.
    - Sections 45-48: Investigation, complaints, international
      cooperation and procedural provisions.

    Complaint forum:
    - FIA Cybercrime / cybercrime authorities.
    - Cyber helpline: 1991.

PART 9 - ISLAMIC LAW PRINCIPLES FOR WOMEN
----------------------------------------------------------------

Islamic law recognizes the following core protections:

- Consent in nikah: Marriage requires consent; coercion is invalid
  and is also criminalized under PPC Section 498B.
- Mehr / dower: A wife is entitled to mehr, prompt or deferred.
- Maintenance: A husband is responsible for maintenance during
  marriage; post-divorce maintenance during iddat is recognized.
- Divorce: A woman may seek khula or judicial divorce under the
  Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act, 1939.
- Inheritance: Muslim women have prescribed inheritance shares.
- Protection from cruelty: Cruelty, violence and failure to maintain
  are grounds for judicial relief and divorce.

Important distinction:
Islamic law provides principles and rights, while Pakistani statutes
and courts determine procedure, evidence, registration and enforcement.

PART 11 - EVIDENCE CHECKLIST
----------------------------------------------------------------

For any case, preserve:

- CNIC copy
- Nikahnama and marriage registration
- Medical reports and medico-legal certificate
- Photographs of injuries or property damage
- Screenshots of messages, posts, profiles and threats
- Original chats and account links
- Call recordings and call logs
- Bank statements and financial records
- Property documents and mutation records
- Witness names and contact details
- Police FIR copy or complaint receipt
- Dates, times and locations of incidents

For online blackmail:
- Do not delete chats, images, URLs or account details.
- Do not pay or negotiate with the blackmailer.
- Report to FIA Cybercrime at 1991.
- File a police complaint if there is physical danger or offline
  contact.
