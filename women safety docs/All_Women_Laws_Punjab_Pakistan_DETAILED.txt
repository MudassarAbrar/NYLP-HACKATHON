PART 1 - PUNJAB PROTECTION OF WOMEN AGAINST VIOLENCE ACT, 2016
================================================================

LAW: Punjab Protection of Women against Violence Act, 2016
ACT NUMBER: Act XVI of 2016
DATE: 29 February 2016
APPLIES TO: Punjab, Pakistan
OFFICIAL FULL TEXT:
https://natlex.ilo.org/dyn/natlex2/natlex2/files/download/102088/PAKD102088.pdf

PURPOSE OF THE LAW
------------------
This is the most important Punjab-specific law for women facing violence.
Its purpose is to create an effective system for:

- Protection of women from violence.
- Relief for women who are victims of violence.
- Rehabilitation of women after violence.
- Rescue of women in danger.
- Medical examination and treatment.
- Legal assistance.
- Shelter and temporary accommodation.
- Counselling and psychological support.
- Prosecution of offenders.
- Monitoring of violence against women in every district.

The Act recognizes that violence against women is not only physical
beating. It also includes emotional abuse, psychological abuse, verbal
abuse, economic abuse, stalking, cybercrime, sexual violence, and
domestic violence.

WHO IS PROTECTED?
-----------------
An "aggrieved person" under this Act is a woman who has suffered violence
from a defendant. The defendant may be:

- Husband.
- Father-in-law.
- Mother-in-law.
- Brother-in-law.
- Sister-in-law.
- Father.
- Mother.
- Brother.
- Sister.
- Son.
- Daughter.
- Employer.
- Acquaintance.
- Neighbour.
- Any other person who commits violence against her.

The law is especially important for domestic-violence cases because it
recognizes that a woman may be abused by people living in the same house,
even where she does not own the house.

TYPES OF VIOLENCE COVERED
-------------------------
The Act covers:

1. Domestic violence:
   Violence by a person with whom the woman lives or has lived in a house
   and who is related by blood, marriage, or adoption.

2. Sexual violence:
   Any sexual offence committed against the woman.

3. Psychological abuse:
   Conduct that causes psychological deterioration, depression, suicidal
   thoughts, anorexia, or loss of freedom of movement.

4. Verbal abuse:
   Insults, humiliation, threats, abusive language, and degrading conduct.

5. Economic abuse:
   Denial of food, clothing, shelter, or taking away the woman's income
   without consent.

6. Stalking:
   Following, watching, contacting, monitoring, or repeatedly trying to
   contact a woman despite her refusal.

7. Cybercrime:
   Online harassment, fake pictures, deepfakes, threats, blackmail,
   hacking, impersonation, and online stalking.

8. Physical violence:
   Beating, hitting, burning, acid attack, confinement, injury, or any
   offence against the human body.

SECTION-BY-SECTION EXPLANATION
------------------------------

SECTION 1: SHORT TITLE, EXTENT AND COMMENCEMENT
-----------------------------------------------
This section gives the law its name: the Punjab Protection of Women
against Violence Act, 2016.

It applies to the whole of Punjab.

It comes into force on the date notified by the Punjab Government. The
Government may notify different dates for different areas if needed.

Practical meaning:
A woman living anywhere in Punjab can seek protection under this law.

SECTION 2: DEFINITIONS
----------------------
This section defines the important words used in the Act.

Important definitions include:

- Aggrieved person:
  A woman who has been subjected to violence.

- Defendant:
  The person against whom the woman seeks protection or relief.

- Domestic violence:
  Violence committed by a person with whom the woman lives or has lived
  in a house and who is related by blood, marriage, or adoption.

- House:
  Any place where the woman lives in a domestic relationship. She does
  not need to own the house. The house may belong to the husband, in-laws,
  or joint family.

- Economic abuse:
  Denial of food, clothing, or shelter according to the defendant's
  financial capacity, or taking the woman's income without her consent.

- Psychological violence:
  Psychological deterioration caused by oppressive behaviour, including
  depression, suicidal attempts, anorexia, or restriction of movement.

- Stalking:
  Following, watching, contacting, or repeatedly trying to contact a
  woman despite her refusal.

- Cybercrime:
  Online offences against a woman.

- Shelter home:
  A government-established or licensed place providing food, lodging,
  protection, and rehabilitation services.

- Protection Centre:
  A one-stop centre for complaint registration, FIR, medical examination,
  forensic evidence, counselling, legal aid, police facilitation, and
  shelter.

- Women Protection Officer:
  A government officer appointed to help women, receive complaints,
  assess danger, coordinate with police and courts, and provide support.

Practical example:
If a husband beats his wife, takes her salary, insults her, threatens her,
prevents her from leaving the house, and sends abusive messages, all of
these acts can fall under this Act.

SECTION 3: MEASURES FOR IMPLEMENTATION
--------------------------------------
The Punjab Government must take practical steps to implement the law.

These include:

- Creating a universal toll-free helpline for women.
- Establishing Protection Centres and shelter homes.
- Appointing staff for rescue, mediation, medical examination,
  psychological treatment, legal help, and investigation.
- Creating awareness in Urdu and local languages.
- Training public servants about violence against women.
- Creating a database and software for monitoring cases.
- Taking other steps needed to protect women.

Practical meaning:
The law does not only create rights on paper. It requires the government
to build institutions and services for women.

SECTION 4: COMPLAINT TO COURT
-----------------------------
A woman can file a complaint directly in the competent Family Court.

She can also file through:

- A Women Protection Officer.
- A person authorized by her.

The complaint can be filed in the court where:

- The woman lives or works.
- The defendant lives or works.
- The woman and defendant last lived together.

PROCEDURE:
- The court must fix the first hearing within seven days of receiving
  the complaint.
- The court issues notice to the defendant.
- The defendant must reply within seven days.
- If the defendant does not reply after proper notice, the court may
  proceed as if he has no valid defence.
- The court must try to decide the complaint within ninety days.

Practical meaning:
This law is designed to be faster than ordinary civil litigation.

SECTION 5: RIGHT TO RESIDE IN HOUSE
-----------------------------------
A woman facing domestic violence cannot be unlawfully evicted from the
house.

If she has ownership, title, or beneficial interest in the house, and she
is wrongfully evicted, the court can restore her position.

She may choose:

- To remain in the house.
- To live in alternative accommodation arranged by the defendant.
- To live in a shelter home.

Practical example:
If in-laws throw a woman out of the matrimonial home, she can approach
the court and seek a residence order.

SECTION 6: INTERIM ORDER
------------------------
During the case, the court can pass an interim order at any stage.

If the court believes that violence has occurred or is likely to occur,
it can issue immediate protection based on:

- The woman's affidavit.
- Medical reports.
- Photographs.
- Messages.
- Witness statements.
- Any other evidence.

Practical meaning:
A woman does not need to wait for the final decision if there is immediate
danger.

SECTION 7: PROTECTION ORDER
---------------------------
If the court is satisfied that violence has occurred or is likely to occur,
it can issue a protection order.

The court may order the defendant:

- Not to communicate with the woman.
- To stay away from her.
- To maintain a specific distance.
- To wear a GPS ankle or wrist tracker in cases of grave violence.
- To move out of the house in grave cases.
- To surrender weapons or firearms.
- Not to buy firearms or obtain a firearm licence.
- Not to aid or abet violence.
- Not to enter the woman's workplace or other places she frequently visits.
- Not to harm her children, relatives, or helpers.
- Not to commit any other act specified by the court.

The court can impose these directions even if the woman did not specifically
ask for them.

The court can also require the defendant to submit a bond, with or without
sureties, to prevent further violence.

Practical meaning:
This is a powerful protective remedy. It can prevent future violence before
it happens.

SECTION 8: RESIDENCE ORDER
--------------------------
In domestic-violence cases, the court can pass a residence order.

The court may order that:

- The woman cannot be unlawfully evicted.
- She has the right to stay in the house.
- She may be moved to a shelter home for protection.
- The defendant must return property or documents she is entitled to.
- The defendant or relatives cannot enter her shelter home, workplace,
  or other places she visits.
- The defendant must arrange alternative accommodation or pay rent.

The court considers the financial capacity of both parties before passing
an order with financial consequences.

Practical example:
If a woman is being forced out of her matrimonial home, the court can
protect her right to residence or arrange alternative accommodation.

SECTION 9: MONETARY ORDER
-------------------------
The court can order the defendant to pay monetary relief to the woman.

This may include:

- Compensation for economic abuse.
- Loss of earnings.
- Medical expenses.
- Loss caused by destruction, damage, or removal of property.
- Rent and meals for shelter if the defendant was legally required to
  provide shelter.
- Maintenance for the woman and her dependent children.
- Other financial relief.

If the defendant does not pay, the court can direct his employer or debtor
to pay a portion of his wages or debt directly to the woman or deposit it
with the court.

Practical meaning:
The law recognizes that abuse often has financial consequences.

SECTION 10: DURATION AND ALTERATION OF ORDERS
---------------------------------------------
Interim, protection, and residence orders remain valid for the period
specified by the court.

The court can alter or discharge an order for reasons recorded in writing.

The woman can apply for discharge of an order.

After discharge, she can file a fresh application if violence occurs again.

Practical meaning:
Protection is not permanent automatically, but it can be renewed or a new
application can be made.

SECTION 11: DISTRICT WOMEN PROTECTION COMMITTEE
-----------------------------------------------
Every district must have a District Women Protection Committee.

It is headed by the District Coordination Officer.

Members include:

- Executive District Officer, Health.
- Executive District Officer, Community Development.
- Representative of District Police.
- District Officer Social Welfare.
- District Public Prosecutor.
- District Women Protection Officer.
- Four non-official members from civil society.

The committee must meet at least once every three months.

Practical meaning:
This committee monitors whether protection services are actually working
in the district.

SECTION 12: FUNCTIONS OF THE COMMITTEE
--------------------------------------
The committee must:

- Supervise Protection Centres, shelter homes, and helplines.
- Ensure the helpline works properly.
- Coordinate with police, health, prosecution, and social-welfare
  departments.
- Mediate and reconcile disputes where appropriate.
- Ensure violence cases reported at police stations are referred to
  Protection Centres.
- Approve annual plans for Protection Centres and shelter homes.
- Monitor performance.
- Enlist women volunteers and organizations.
- Suggest reforms to the government.
- Ensure minimum standards and codes of conduct.
- Prepare annual reports.

Practical meaning:
The committee is responsible for making the protection system functional
at district level.

SECTION 13: PROTECTION CENTRES AND SHELTER HOMES
------------------------------------------------
The government must establish Protection Centres in phases.

A Protection Centre provides:

- Police reporting.
- FIR registration.
- Medical examination.
- Forensic evidence collection.
- First aid.
- Immediate protection.
- Legal aid.
- Psychological counselling.
- Mediation.
- Complaint registration.
- Community guidance.

A shelter home provides:

- Food and lodging.
- Protection for women and dependent children.
- Rehabilitation services.
- Technical and vocational training.
- Legal, medical, emotional, and psychological support.
- Repatriation or rehabilitation where possible.

Practical meaning:
A woman can receive multiple services at one place instead of running
between police, hospital, court, and shelter.

SECTION 14: WOMEN PROTECTION OFFICER
------------------------------------
The government appoints a District Women Protection Officer for each
district and Women Protection Officers.

Their duties include:

- Supervising the protection system.
- Maintaining liaison with police, health, and other departments.
- Training staff.
- Counselling women.
- Approving rescue operations.
- Filing habeas corpus cases where a woman is wrongfully confined.
- Setting targets and monitoring performance.
- Responding to calls and online queries.
- Rescuing women in danger.
- Admitting women and children to shelter homes.
- Directing a defendant to leave the house for up to forty-eight hours
  in urgent cases.
- Supervising shelter-home staff.

Practical meaning:
The Women Protection Officer is the main government officer a woman can
approach for help under this Act.

SECTION 15: POWER TO ENTER
--------------------------
A Women Protection Officer can enter a house or place to rescue a woman,
but she cannot rescue the woman without her consent.

The officer must give reasonable notice to the person in charge of the
house. If access is denied, the officer can enter with district authorities
and police.

Only a female officer can exercise the power of entry into a woman's house
or place of residence.

Practical meaning:
This provision allows rescue in serious cases while protecting the woman's
consent and dignity.

SECTION 16: ASSISTANCE ON REQUEST
---------------------------------
A Women Protection Officer must provide reasonable assistance to a woman
who requests help.

Assistance can be provided based on:

- The woman's request.
- Information received from any source.
- A complaint.

However, assistance cannot be forced on a woman who voluntarily refuses it.

Practical meaning:
The woman has the right to accept or refuse assistance.

SECTION 17: ASSISTANCE TO OFFICERS
----------------------------------
Police, local government, and other government agencies must assist Women
Protection Officers.

The District Women Protection Committee can demand information from any
government agency or local government.

Practical meaning:
The law creates coordination between different departments.

SECTION 18: PENALTY FOR OBSTRUCTING A PROTECTION OFFICER
---------------------------------------------------------
Any person who obstructs a Women Protection Officer can be punished with:

- Imprisonment up to six months.
- Fine up to Rs 500,000.
- Or both.

Practical meaning:
Family members, employers, or others cannot legally block a protection
officer from performing official duties.

SECTION 19: PENALTY FOR FALSE COMPLAINT
---------------------------------------
A person who knowingly gives false information about violence can be
punished with:

- Imprisonment up to three months.
- Fine between Rs 50,000 and Rs 100,000.
- Or both.

Practical meaning:
The law protects both genuine victims and defendants from false accusations.

SECTION 20: PENALTY FOR BREACH OF ORDERS
----------------------------------------
If a defendant violates an interim order, protection order, residence
order, or monetary order, or interferes with a GPS tracker, he can be
punished with:

- Imprisonment up to one year.
- Fine between Rs 50,000 and Rs 200,000.
- Or both.

If he violates the order more than once:

- Imprisonment between one and two years.
- Fine between Rs 100,000 and Rs 500,000.
- Or both.

Practical meaning:
Court orders have real consequences.

SECTION 21: COGNIZANCE AND SUMMARY TRIAL
----------------------------------------
The court can take cognizance of an offence under this Act only on a
complaint by the District Women Protection Officer or a Women Protection
Officer acting on her behalf.

The trial is conducted according to summary-trial procedures.

Practical meaning:
The process is intended to be quicker than ordinary criminal trials.

SECTION 22: APPEAL
------------------
A person aggrieved by an interim, protection, residence, or monetary order,
or by a sentence, can appeal to the Court of Session within thirty days.

The Court of Session must decide the appeal within sixty days.

Practical meaning:
Both parties have a right to appeal, but the process is time-bound.

SECTION 23: RIGHT TO INFORMATION
--------------------------------
The government must publish information about violence against women and
steps taken for protection on its website.

However, information can be withheld to protect:

- The woman's safety, privacy, dignity, or security.
- National security or public order.
- An ongoing investigation.
- The identity of an officer at risk.

Practical meaning:
Transparency is required, but the woman's privacy is protected.

SECTION 24: PUBLIC SERVANTS
---------------------------
People administering this Act are considered public servants under the
Pakistan Penal Code.

Practical meaning:
They can be held accountable for misuse of office.

SECTION 25: PERFORMANCE AUDIT
-----------------------------
The government must periodically audit the performance of the protection
system.

The audit reviews:

- Quality of services.
- Targets achieved.
- Weaknesses.
- Recommendations for improvement.

Practical meaning:
The law requires evaluation, not just establishment of institutions.

SECTION 26: ACT NOT IN DEROGATION OF OTHER LAWS
-----------------------------------------------
This Act is in addition to other laws.

A woman can also use:

- Pakistan Penal Code.
- PECA.
- Family laws.
- Constitution.
- Other applicable laws.

Practical meaning:
She can choose multiple legal remedies where appropriate.

SECTION 27: TRAINING
--------------------
The government must train Women Protection Officers and other staff.

Practical meaning:
Officials must be trained to handle violence cases sensitively.

SECTION 28: ANNUAL REPORT
-------------------------
The government must submit an annual report to the Punjab Assembly.

The report must include:

- Services provided.
- Rescue operations.
- Delays and reasons for delay.
- Performance audits.
- Recommendations for reform.

Practical meaning:
Parliamentary oversight is built into the law.

SECTION 29: RULES
-----------------
The government must make rules for implementation, including rules on:

- Monitoring and evaluation.
- Toll-free helpline.
- Committee meetings.
- Women volunteers.
- Donations.
- Medical, legal, and psychological assistance.
- Shelter facilities.
- Repatriation and rehabilitation.
- Record-keeping.
- Protection Centres and shelter homes.

SECTION 30: IMMUNITY
--------------------
Government officers, committee members, Women Protection Officers, and
protection-system staff are protected from legal action for good-faith
acts done under the Act.

Practical meaning:
Officials can perform duties without fear of frivolous litigation, but
this does not protect bad-faith or illegal conduct.

SECTION 31: REMOVAL OF DIFFICULTIES
-----------------------------------
The government can issue notifications to remove difficulties in
implementing the Act.

PART 2 - PUNJAB WOMEN PROTECTION AUTHORITY ACT, 2017
================================================================

LAW: Punjab Women Protection Authority Act, 2017
SOURCE:
https://jppbenchbook.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/11/The-Punjab-Women-Protection-Authority-Act-2017.pdf

PURPOSE
-------
This law establishes the Punjab Women Protection Authority.

Its purpose is to make the women-protection system stronger, coordinated,
and accountable.

MAIN FUNCTIONS
--------------
The Authority works on:

- Policy for women's protection.
- Monitoring implementation of women-protection laws.
- Coordination between departments.
- Oversight of Protection Centres and shelter homes.
- Training and capacity-building.
- Awareness campaigns.
- Data collection and evaluation.
- Recommendations for legal and administrative reform.
- Support for district-level protection committees.

WHY IT MATTERS
--------------
The Punjab Protection of Women against Violence Act, 2016 creates the
protection system. The Punjab Women Protection Authority Act, 2017 creates
the institution responsible for overseeing and improving that system.

PART 5 - PROTECTION AGAINST HARASSMENT OF WOMEN AT THE WORKPLACE ACT, 2010
================================================================

LAW: Protection Against Harassment of Women at the Workplace Act, 2010
AMENDED: 2022
OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://fospah.gov.pk/SiteImage/Misc/files/PAHAW_amend_2022.pdf

PURPOSE
-------
This law protects women from harassment at work.

It applies to:

- Government offices.
- Private companies.
- Factories.
- Hospitals.
- Universities.
- Schools.
- Colleges.
- NGOs.
- Media organizations.
- Other workplaces.

It also applies to educational institutions.

WHAT IS HARASSMENT?
-------------------
Harassment includes:

- Unwelcome sexual advances.
- Requests for sexual favours.
- Sexual comments or jokes.
- Sexual messages or calls.
- Physical contact without consent.
- Staring or sexual gestures.
- Threats related to job, grades, promotion, or employment.
- Creating a hostile work environment.
- Any verbal, non-verbal, written, or physical conduct of a sexual
  nature that affects dignity or work performance.

IMPORTANT: Harassment does not require physical touching. Words, messages,
emails, gestures, and abuse of authority can also be harassment.

SECTION-BY-SECTION EXPLANATION
------------------------------

SECTION 2: DEFINITIONS
----------------------
This section defines:

- Harassment.
- Workplace.
- Employee.
- Employer.
- Complainant.
- Respondent.
- Inquiry Committee.
- Competent Authority.
- Ombudsperson.

SECTION 3: INQUIRY COMMITTEE
----------------------------
Every organization must constitute an Inquiry Committee.

The committee is responsible for:

- Receiving complaints.
- Conducting inquiries.
- Maintaining confidentiality.
- Recommending penalties.
- Protecting complainants from retaliation.

SECTION 4: EMPLOYER RESPONSIBILITIES
------------------------------------
The employer must:

- Constitute an Inquiry Committee.
- Display the code of conduct.
- Create awareness among employees.
- Take complaints seriously.
- Protect complainants.
- Take disciplinary action against offenders.

SECTION 5: COMPLAINT PROCEDURE
------------------------------
A woman can complain to:

- The Inquiry Committee.
- Any member of the Inquiry Committee.
- The Ombudsperson.

The complaint should include:

- Name of complainant.
- Name of respondent.
- Date, time, and place of incident.
- Description of harassment.
- Witnesses.
- Evidence such as messages, emails, call recordings, or screenshots.
- Relief sought.

SECTION 6: POWERS OF INQUIRY COMMITTEE
--------------------------------------
The Inquiry Committee can:

- Summon witnesses.
- Collect evidence.
- Record statements.
- Examine documents.
- Recommend disciplinary action.
- Recommend compensation or other remedies.

SECTION 7: PENALTIES
--------------------
If harassment is proved, penalties may include:

- Warning.
- Fine.
- Withholding promotion.
- Demotion.
- Suspension.
- Dismissal.
- Termination of contract.

SECTION 8: COMPLAINT TO OMBUDSPERSON
------------------------------------
If there is no Inquiry Committee, or the woman is not satisfied with the
committee's decision, she can complain to FOSPAH.

FOSPAH is the Federal Ombudsperson Secretariat for Protection Against
Harassment.

FOSPAH is a quasi-judicial body and can issue legally binding decisions.

CONTACT:
- Helpline: 051-9218845
- Email: complaints@fospah.gov.pk

PART 6 - PAKISTAN PENAL CODE, 1860: WOMEN-PROTECTION SECTIONS
================================================================

LAW: Pakistan Penal Code, 1860
OFFICIAL SOURCE:
https://pakistancode.gov.pk/pdffiles/administratord5622ea3f15bfa00b17d2cf7770a8434.pdf

The PPC is the main criminal law of Pakistan. It applies in Punjab.

Below are the sections most relevant to women.

SECTION 294: OBSCENE ACTS AND SONGS
-----------------------------------
This section punishes obscene acts or songs in or near a public place,
causing annoyance to others.

Relevance:
It can apply to public sexual harassment, obscene behaviour, or obscene
performances near women.

SECTION 324: ATTEMPTED MURDER
-----------------------------
This section applies where a person attempts to cause death.

Relevance:
It may apply in serious acid attacks, shootings, stabbing, or other
violent attacks where death could have resulted.

SECTION 326 / 326A-326B: DANGEROUS HURT AND ACID CRIME
-------------------------------------------------------
These provisions deal with causing hurt by dangerous weapons or means.

Relevance:
They apply to acid attacks, burning, poisoning, and other serious physical
violence.

SECTION 337: HURT
-----------------
This section deals with causing hurt or injury to a person.

Relevance:
It applies to physical assault, beating, and injury.

SECTION 337E: ACID CRIME
------------------------
This section specifically deals with acid crimes.

Relevance:
It applies where acid is thrown or administered with intent to injure,
disfigure, or harm a person.

SECTION 354: ASSAULT OR CRIMINAL FORCE TO WOMAN WITH INTENT TO OUTRAGE
HER MODESTY
-----------------------------------------------------------------------
This section applies where a person assaults or uses criminal force against
a woman with intent to outrage her modesty.

Relevance:
It covers physical sexual assault short of rape.

SECTION 354A: ASSAULT OR USE OF CRIMINAL FORCE TO WOMAN AND STRIPPING HER
OF CLOTHES
--------------------------------------------------------------------------
This section applies where a person assaults a woman or uses criminal force
and strips her or attempts to strip her of clothes.

Relevance:
It covers public stripping, forced nakedness, and related humiliation.

SECTION 354B: VITIATING ATMOSPHERE FOR WOMAN
--------------------------------------------
This section deals with conduct intended to insult or harm the dignity or
safety environment of women.

Relevance:
It can apply to conduct that creates fear, humiliation, or insecurity for
women.

SECTION 354C: VOYEURISM
-----------------------
This section applies where a person watches or captures images of a woman
engaging in a private act without her consent.

Relevance:
It covers hidden cameras, secret recording, bathroom/bedroom recording,
and non-consensual private filming.

SECTION 354D: STALKING
----------------------
This section applies where a person follows, contacts, monitors, or
attempts to contact a woman despite clear disinterest.

It includes electronic stalking.

Relevance:
It applies to repeated calls, messages, following, surveillance, and
online tracking.

SECTION 365-365B: KIDNAPPING AND ABDUCTION
------------------------------------------
These sections deal with kidnapping and abduction.

Relevance:
They apply where a woman is kidnapped, abducted, confined, or taken away
for marriage, exploitation, or other illegal purposes.

SECTION 366A: PROCURATION OF MINOR GIRL
---------------------------------------
This section applies where a person induces or forces a minor girl into
illicit intercourse or exploitation.

Relevance:
It protects underage girls from sexual exploitation.

SECTION 366B: IMPORTATION OF GIRL FROM FOREIGN COUNTRY
------------------------------------------------------
This section applies where a girl under twenty-one is imported into
Pakistan for illicit intercourse or related exploitation.

SECTION 375-376: RAPE AND PUNISHMENT
------------------------------------
Section 375 defines rape.

Section 376 provides punishment for rape.

Relevance:
These are the main provisions for sexual violence.

SECTION 376A-376E: AGGRAVATED RAPE OFFENCES
-------------------------------------------
These provisions cover:

- Gang rape.
- Custodial rape.
- Rape causing death.
- Other aggravated circumstances.

SECTION 493: COHABITATION CAUSED BY DECEITFUL MEANS
----------------------------------------------------
This section applies where a man deceives a woman into a relationship by
pretending to be her husband.

Relevance:
It protects women from fraudulent relationships based on false marriage
claims.

SECTION 494: BIGAMY
-------------------
This section applies where a person marries again during the subsistence
of a lawful marriage, subject to legal exceptions.

Relevance:
It can apply in fraudulent second-marriage cases.

SECTION 495: CONCEALMENT OF FORMER MARRIAGE
-------------------------------------------
This section applies where a person conceals a former marriage from the
person with whom a subsequent marriage is contracted.

SECTION 496: MARRIAGE CEREMONY FRAUDULENTLY GONE THROUGH
--------------------------------------------------------
This section applies where a person dishonestly goes through a marriage
ceremony knowing that no lawful marriage will result.

SECTION 496A: FICTITIOUS MARRIAGE CEREMONY
------------------------------------------
This section applies where a person dishonestly or fraudulently goes
through a marriage ceremony knowing it is not a lawful marriage.

Relevance:
It applies to fake nikah ceremonies and fraudulent marriage claims.

SECTION 497: ADULTERY-RELATED PROVISION
---------------------------------------
This is a historic provision. Its legal treatment has changed through
judicial interpretation and later law.

SECTION 498: ENTICING OR TAKING AWAY A MARRIED WOMAN
----------------------------------------------------
This section applies where a person entices, takes away, or detains a
married woman with criminal intent.

SECTION 498A: KIDNAPPING OR ABDUCTING WOMAN TO COMPEL MARRIAGE
---------------------------------------------------------------
This section applies where a woman is kidnapped, abducted, or compelled
to marry.

SECTION 498B: PROHIBITION OF FORCED MARRIAGE
--------------------------------------------
This is one of the most important sections for women.

It criminalizes forcing or coercing a woman into marriage.

PUNISHMENT:
- 3 to 7 years imprisonment.
- Fine up to Rs 500,000.

If the victim is a girl child or non-Muslim woman:
- 5 to 10 years imprisonment.
- Fine up to Rs 1,000,000.

Practical meaning:
Parents, guardians, relatives, or any other person cannot legally force
a woman to marry.

SECTION 506: CRIMINAL INTIMIDATION
----------------------------------
This section applies where a person threatens another with injury, harm,
or other consequences.

Relevance:
It applies to threats, blackmail, and intimidation.

SECTION 509: INSULTING MODESTY AND SEXUAL HARASSMENT
----------------------------------------------------
This section covers words, gestures, or acts intended to insult a woman's
modesty, sexual harassment, and related intrusive conduct.

Relevance:
It applies to public harassment, sexual comments, obscene gestures, and
intrusive behaviour.

SECTION 499-500: DEFAMATION
---------------------------
These sections deal with false imputations that harm a person's reputation.

Relevance:
They can apply to false accusations, humiliating posts, and reputation
attacks.

SECTION 463-477A: FORGERY AND FORGED DOCUMENTS
----------------------------------------------
These sections deal with forgery, forged documents, and fraudulent use of
documents.

Relevance:
They apply to:

- Fake nikahnama.
- Fake divorce papers.
- Fake property documents.
- Fake CNIC copies.
- Fake consent documents.
- Fraudulent transfer deeds.

PART 23 - ISLAMIC LAW PRINCIPLES FOR WOMEN
================================================================

Islamic law provides several core protections for women.

1. CONSENT IN MARRIAGE
----------------------
Islamic marriage requires consent.

Coercion invalidates consent.

Forced marriage is also criminalized under PPC Section 498B.

2. MEHR / DOWER
---------------
A wife is entitled to mehr.

Mehr may be prompt or deferred.

It is a marital financial right, not a discretionary gift.

3. MAINTENANCE
--------------
A husband is responsible for maintenance during marriage.

After divorce, maintenance during iddat is recognized.

Pakistani courts have also addressed longer-term maintenance in certain
circumstances.

4. DIVORCE RIGHTS
-----------------
A woman may seek divorce through:

- Khula.
- Judicial dissolution under the Dissolution of Muslim Marriages Act,
  1939.

Grounds include cruelty, desertion, failure to maintain, and other
legally recognized grounds.

5. INHERITANCE
--------------
Muslim women have prescribed inheritance shares.

A daughter, wife, mother, or sister is entitled to her share.

Denial of inheritance is criminalized under the Prevention of Anti-Women
Practices Act, 2011.

6. PROTECTION FROM CRUELTY
--------------------------
Cruelty, violence, and failure to maintain are grounds for judicial
relief and divorce.

IMPORTANT DISTINCTION
---------------------
Islamic law provides principles and rights.

Pakistani statutes and courts determine procedure, evidence, registration,
and enforcement.

Where cultural practice denies a woman her mehr, inheritance, consent,
or freedom from coercion, it may contradict both Islamic principles and
Pakistani law.
